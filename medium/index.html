Medium Readings

A medium reading is for navigating loss and seeking connection or closure — not for general life questions. The category deserves particular care: grief is real, vulnerability is high, and the difference between a responsible medium and a manipulative one is wide.

What it does

A medium reading, in one paragraph

A medium attempts to connect with someone who has died and convey messages or a sense of presence to the living person. Whether you treat this as literal or symbolic, the value people report is often less about “facts” and more about a felt sense of connection, meaning, or things-left-unsaid finally reaching expression.

The short version

A medium reading fits when you are navigating loss and want connection or closure. It does not fit for general life questions (try psychic), reflective pattern-spotting (try tarot), or timing (try astrology).

The key distinction

Psychic vs medium — the difference that matters

These are not the same service. A psychic reads your situation; a medium attempts to reach someone who has passed. Not all psychics are mediums, and not all mediums read current life situations well.

Who it's for

It fits when…

  • You are navigating grief and want a sense of connection, not necessarily facts.
  • There are things left unsaid and you want a space for that, regardless of literal truth.
  • You're past the acute stage and considering meaning-making, not crisis support.

It does not fit when…

  • You are in acute crisis — a medium is not a substitute for grief counseling or therapy.
  • You want a guarantee of contact. No responsible medium guarantees a specific connection.
  • You're seeking general life guidance — that's psychic, tarot, or astrology.
Questions to ask

What to bring to a medium reading

Approach the session with

  • An open name or initial of the person — not a flood of biographical detail. Let the medium bring specifics, not you.
  • One or two things left unsaid you'd want to express or hear acknowledged.
  • Permission to yourself that the session may not produce the contact you hoped for — and that's not a verdict on the person you lost.
  • A support person or follow-up plan, because readings can surface grief you didn't expect.
How to choose

How to choose a medium — with extra care

Because the emotional stakes are high, the vetting bar is higher than for other categories.

  1. Look for evidence-based specifics — a responsible medium offers verifiable details you didn't supply, not generic “I sense a father figure.”
  2. Watch for cold reading — fishing questions (“does the letter M mean anything?”) that put words in your mouth.
  3. Refuse “curse” or “blockage” upsells — the most common fraud pattern in this category.
  4. Read the refund policy before booking — reputable mediums acknowledge sessions vary.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed contact with a specific person.
  • “Energy clearing” or “curse removal” upsells attached to the session.
  • Pressure to book longer or repeat sessions for “the connection to hold.”
  • Refusal to let you bring a support person.
